# Methods: Model Efficiency Residual and Know-How Index

Baseline snapshot 2026-10-06; Le Chonk token supplement reviewed 2026-10-08.
Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3.2, free API v4.3.
Authoritative analysis inputs are the curated CSV, source registry and saved API response.
The raw API has a SHA-256 guard; CSV scores/dates must match the saved response.
Versions, parameter ordering, ranges, token provenance and core inclusion are validated.

## Sample

55 catalogue rows, 35 core observations, 19 core families, 11 represented labs.
Core excludes unknown active counts, independent size summaries, estimated or unverified
benchmark measurement status and models whose weights are not available. One preselected
effort setting per release avoids mixing inference settings as architecture replications.
Le Chonk is held out; technical documentation checked 2026-10-07 gives 1,050B total /
52B active, while the launch announcement says 49B. Retain a 49–52B active range and
use technical docs' 52B for the preferred point. Endpoint residuals are exported; the
small discrepancy does not change the conclusion. Preview weights unavailable at snapshot.
For any finite model-size discrepancy range, 10,000 uniform parameter draws are paired
with family-bootstrap baselines. Model residual 5th/50th/95th scenario percentiles are
exported for every identifiable specification. Uniformity is an explicit modeling assumption;
these 90% scenario ranges do not replace 95% baseline intervals or missing score variance.
The October 8 public comparison displays all ten evaluations without an estimated-score
marker. A fresh authenticated API check confirms the same model ID, name, slug, release
date and exact 38.4 score. That score still comes from the original October 6 snapshot.

This is a purposive catalogue, not all known architectures. Missing token tables also
restrict measurement confirmation; the same 35 observations support primary S/C/F comparisons.
The reasoning-only subset has 29 observations, 16 families; zero-reasoning models are
excluded from its log transform, while missing reasoning is not assigned zero.
Historical architectures have current-version scores; historical incompatible indices are absent.

## Specifications

KHI means Know-How Index: observed Intelligence Index minus the fitted expectation.
The name is a working label, not a direct measure of skill. Model Efficiency Residual
is the preferred interpretation. KHI-S is the Size-adjusted Know-How Index; KHI-C is
the Compute-adjusted Know-How Index (an inference proxy); KHI-F is the Fuller-resource
Know-How Index. F excludes actual training compute. SEM means Scale Efficiency Multiplier.
MoE means mixture of experts; FLOPs means floating-point operations; RMSE means root
mean squared error. HC3 is a heteroskedasticity correction; AIC and BIC are information
criteria that penalize additional fitted terms and require matched samples for comparison.

Ordinary least squares, scores in original index points. Base-10 logs; parameter counts
in billions; task-token counts as published rounded averages. Time = years since 2026-01-01
using elapsed days / 365.25. Intercepts depend on these units; residuals do not.

- S: score = a + b log10(active).
- Capacity: S + c log10(total). Also compare S/capacity on MoE-only sample.
- Time: S + e time.
- C: S + d log10(total generated output). This includes answer and reasoning expenditure.
- Reasoning: S + d log10(reasoning), positive-reasoning subset only.
- Product: score = a + b log10(active × output), and similarly for positive reasoning.
  These impose equal coefficients on size and token logs.
- F: S + c log10(total) + d log10(output) + e time. Exploratory, five coefficients.
- Zero-reasoning diagnostics: log(reasoning + 1) and log(reasoning + 1,000).

Main C is chosen for interpretable independent coefficients, common coverage and avoidance
of arbitrary zero offsets, not merely the highest fitted R². F remains exploratory.
OLS is descriptive: no regressions identify causal effects or matched training budgets.
Dense and MoE models need not share a universal curve; parameter activation is not exact FLOPs.

## Residuals and equivalents

Raw KHI = score − prediction. z = raw / sqrt(SSE/(n−p)); normalized score = 100 + 15z.
Primary interpretation: performance relative to observable resource proxies.
S/C/F use their own fitted-sample residual SD, so standardized scores are baseline-dependent.

Given the S curve, log10(P_equivalent) = (score−a)/b, and SEM = 10^(raw/b).
Equivalent size is never a proprietary size estimate. Uncertainty is generated by the
same family bootstrap. If the slope's 95% interval reaches zero, inversions are labelled
unbounded and finite interval endpoints remain null. Zero/negative draws are not dropped.
Point equivalents are retained for audit; all current frontier equivalents extrapolate
far beyond the observed active range. SEM is therefore not the headline metric.

## Uncertainty and diagnostics

OLS exports R², adjusted R², residual SD, RMSE, AIC and BIC (Gaussian constant omitted),
condition number, leverage and Cook's distance. AIC/BIC are compared only on equal samples.
Prediction checks: ordinary leave-one-observation-out and leave-one-family-out RMSE,
weighted by the number of held-out observations. Each fold refits coefficients.

HC3 uses (X'X)^−1 X' diag((residual/(1−leverage))²) X (X'X)^−1.
Cluster sandwich covariance sums outer products of group scores X_g' residual_g,
with correction G/(G−1) × (n−1)/(n−p). Family and lab SEs are both exported.
Few clusters can still make asymptotic inference unreliable; no lab-skill p-value leaderboard.

Main intervals: 2,000 family-block percentile bootstrap draws, seed 20261006. A draw
samples G families with replacement and includes all observations in each selected family.
Singular draws are rejected and the valid count is exported. Point/curve bands quantify
uncertainty in the fitted mean, conditional on fixed benchmark scores, not task-level score noise.
Residual intervals hold the target's resource vector and benchmark score fixed. They are
not out-of-sample predictive intervals; overlap does not establish a rank ordering.

Lab means and medians use S residuals from the global baseline, including own observations.
Recent (cutoff 2025-04-06) and reasoning subgroup means restrict the same residuals.
For labs with >=3 families, intervals additionally resample their families and recompute
mean residuals under sampled global baselines. This is a descriptive uncertainty summary,
not evidence that all other confounders have been removed. Fewer families yields null intervals.

Sensitivities: recent 18 months, positive reasoning, one latest/largest representative per
family without score-based selection, official-only sizes, added independent sizes,
added unverified/estimated scores (explicitly weaker evidence), cited alternative module
accounting. Family jackknife removes each entire family in turn. No influential observation
is silently excluded. Finite parameter ranges are sampled uniformly in a separate 1,000-draw
measurement-accounting sensitivity; token rounding is sampled ±500 in 1,000 further draws.
These distributions are assumptions, not calibrated probabilities of architecture error.

Closed scenarios, when credible sources exist, use a specified uniform or log-uniform
size range and sampled S baselines for 10,000 simulations. Report residual 5th/50th/95th
percentiles. Current scenario table is empty: unavailable is distinct from zero uncertainty.

## Missing analyses and interpretation

Seven representative models have rounded constituent results transcribed from cited public
tables. This partial set is descriptive and not used for benchmark-level regression.
No task-level benchmark variance, complete numeric ten-evaluation dataset, consistent
training-FLOP history or reliable current proprietary parameter estimates is available.
Free API capability indices are not a complete constituent-evaluation decomposition.
Le Chonk's October 8 token supplement enables held-out C/F residuals. Proprietary active
sizes and complete inference work remain unknown, so a matched frontier compute gap
is still unavailable.
Training data, compute, routing, tokenization, distillation, RL, verifiers, contamination,
hidden inference, benchmark optimization, release timing and errors all enter the residual.

The evidence supports substantial unexplained efficiency differences (B), with insufficient
information to identify scale versus lab skill causally (C). It does not support active
size alone explaining frontier variation (A) in this catalogue. That statement does not
reject controlled within-family scaling laws.

## Separate training-resource audit

The public training-disclosure JSON records 12 catalogue models and one proprietary context row, including 9 core rows.
Seven core rows disclose exact pretraining-token budgets, spanning four families and
three laboratories. Two core rows disclose exact accelerator-hours, using different
hardware and stage scopes. None supplies a verified comparable complete training-FLOP
total. These observations do not enter the regression predictors or change core inclusion.

Reported pretraining-token budgets measure data exposure. GPU-hours combine device
count with run duration, but H100 and H800 hours are not interchangeable units of
arithmetic work. TPU chip counts lack duration and achieved utilization. Hardware peak
throughput is not substituted for measured training compute. Scope notes retain research,
context extension, teacher, vision and posttraining exclusions. Approximate relative
gpt-oss-20b hours remain missing numerically; Llama 4 token counts remain approximate.

No common training-adjusted residual is reported. Multiplying inference active parameters
by training tokens and a generic constant would omit architecture-specific training work,
teacher costs and later reinforcement-learning stages. The disclosures expose an important
confounder without pretending that an equal-budget comparison has been established.
Each row links a creator disclosure or a firsthand account of a company briefing. Exact source/version mappings
avoid transferring a preview model's budget to a later release without explicit evidence.

## October 8 Le Chonk token supplement

`mistral-token-supplement.json` records 69,000 output tokens per weighted benchmark task,
including 52,000 reasoning tokens, from the public Mistral Large 4 / Step 5 table under
v4.3.2. Reasoning is included in total output: the counts must not be added. The fresh
API model observation is stored without credentials. Its precise score and identity match
the October 6 observation, while the table supplies the patch version and rounded tokens.
The pipeline rejects changed configuration identity, score, version, counts, provenance,
or accidental inclusion of the preview in the fitted sample. The verification record has a
SHA-256 guard. Core rows, all twelve regression fits, and their bootstrap distributions
remain unchanged. Matching names and scores cannot independently rule out hidden
changes to a preview; later retrieval is explicit rather than backdated.

At 52B active, 1,050B total and score 38.4, the held-out predictions are S 24.6898,
C 35.4988 and F 38.2584. Residuals are +13.7102, +2.9012 and +0.1416.
Conditional 95% family-bootstrap baseline intervals are respectively [6.5898, 20.7587],
[-2.2260, 9.7747] and [-4.3530, 4.8528]. C/F include zero; these intervals describe
baseline uncertainty at fixed inputs and score, not an equal-budget or causal result.

Output rounding endpoints at 68,500 and 69,500 are exported for C/F. The joint input
scenario samples the active-count discrepancy uniformly over 49–52B and the output
rounding bin uniformly over 68,500–69,500, independently of family-bootstrap baselines,
for 10,000 draws. Total size, date and score remain fixed. Its 5th–95th residual ranges
are 90% scenario percentiles under explicit assumptions, distinct from the 95% baseline
intervals; neither includes missing benchmark-score variance or unobserved training work.

Mistral Large 4 reports from-scratch training on 3,800 Grace Blackwell GPUs;
firsthand press-conference reporting gives approximately two months (and rounds the
cluster to 4,000). A constant 60-day, full-allocation scenario yields 5.472M scheduled
GPU-hours; measured GPU-hours remain null. Astra reports more than 100,000 GPUs,
stored as a strict lower threshold with duration and chip type unspecified by the cited
interview. These disclosures describe training runs, not separately budgeted benchmark
effort configurations. Index points per 1,000 reported GPUs are descriptive arithmetic,
not a ratio-scale intelligence or total-training-efficiency metric.

Distillation transfers teacher behavior through training targets or generated examples.
Gemma 3 documents pretraining distillation; Nemotron 3 Ultra documents multi-teacher
on-policy distillation. Student-only compute omits teacher training and generation;
full-lifecycle and incremental deployment/training costs answer different questions.
From-scratch initialization does not imply no synthetic or teacher-generated data.
