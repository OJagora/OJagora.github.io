AI / Research note

Is AI use growing
exponentially?

OpenAI says its researchers’ agent use is growing exponentially. What do the numbers from another lab and a public platform show?

November 19, 2026

OpenAI says agent use in its research organization has continued to grow exponentially. Anthropic and OpenRouter also report extraordinary increases. The available evidence establishes rapid growth across these settings. It is less conclusive about whether each series follows a stable exponential curve.

OpenAI’s researchers

OpenAI reports that the median researcher’s daily agent usage, valued at public API list prices, rose from about $1,500 in September to $2,400 in October 2026. At the 90th percentile, it rose from about $11,000 to $23,000. OpenAI reports increases of 62% and 105%, respectively. OpenAI, November 2026

Its average agent runtime also rose from 0.5 to 5.7 eight-hour agent workdays per researcher per day over the reported series. Agents can run concurrently, and the metric generally covers interactive use. Both measures point to much more intensive use. The expenditure figures also reflect model choice and changes in agent systems, and represent API-equivalent value rather than actual internal cost.

The daily series shows a steep rise through summer, a dip following a temporary harness efficiency change and another sharp increase in October. These changes make the growth rate uneven across the period.

Anthropic’s internal use

Anthropic reports that the list-price value of its internal Claude use grew by more than 250 times from January to September 2026. That is company-wide and combines changes in the organization and its usage. As a mechanical illustration, a constant exponential rate producing exactly 250× over eight months would be about 99% per month. The endpoint figures do not tell us whether monthly growth actually followed that path. Anthropic filing, page 183

A separate Anthropic study reports that employee-directed interactive agents ran for about six times the assumed human work hours by August. Its runtime includes inference and active tool use, pools subagents and excludes waits longer than 30 minutes. This is another sign of growing agent activity, measured differently from OpenAI’s daily expenditure. Anthropic, How employment is changing

OpenRouter’s public platform

OpenRouter reports 790 trillion prompt and completion tokens in October, up 37% from September and almost 25 times January’s volume. Agentic API keys accounted for 76% of October tokens, compared with 8% a year earlier. Its classification applies to API keys, so it describes traffic patterns rather than a count of autonomous agents or individual users. OpenRouter, October 2026 recap

These are platform totals. They can grow because more customers join, existing customers use more tokens, or workloads and model choices change. OpenRouter also reports effective token prices falling since January, which can encourage greater use. Platform growth therefore cannot on its own show how much the typical person’s use grew or how the access gap changed.

Vercel’s AI Gateway

Vercel reports that estimated gateway spending in September 2026 was roughly 13 times its December 2025 level, and 3.43 times its April level. The median paid customer active in both December and September more than doubled its spending. Those results show growth across the platform and within a continuing customer cohort. Vercel, AI Gateway Production Index

Vercel estimates spending at published list prices, so bills can differ. Its token count includes input, output, reasoning and cached tokens. Platform totals can change with the number of customers and the mix of models and workloads.

What can we conclude?

AI use is rising rapidly in frontier labs and public inference platforms. All four measures rose sharply over their observed periods, though growth rates varied. OpenAI’s median usage rose about 97-fold from early April to October 18, OpenRouter’s monthly tokens rose about 8.1-fold from April to October, and Vercel’s estimated spending rose about 3.4-fold from April to September. Anthropic’s interactive agent hours per human hour rose about 9.6-fold from early April to early August. OpenAI and Anthropic values are approximate chart readings. These measures cover different populations and workloads, and their growth rates do not establish a common exponential trend.
